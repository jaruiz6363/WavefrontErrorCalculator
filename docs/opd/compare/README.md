Optiland and OSLO appear only in the aiming-off tables: neither was gathered with real ray aiming.

**WEC's W** is Hopkins's reference sphere through the real exit pupil, centred where the chief ray meets the image. Its rays are launched as the programs launch theirs, so that each row is the same ray:
- for the aiming-off tables, unaimed, at the paraxial entrance pupil (OpticStudio's ray aiming Off); for the aiming-real tables, aimed at the real stop (OpticStudio's Real). OpticStudio's third setting, ray aiming Paraxial, was not tested;
- the chief ray through the stop centre;
- pupil coordinates oriented as OpticStudio orients them.

**Every column is on the same glass:** the refractive indices OpticStudio uses, from the AGF catalogs, identical at every surface and wavelength (to 5×10⁻¹⁵). W is computed on them. Left to themselves, two programs would use other values:
- **Double Gauss:** the file names `F4` with no catalog. OpticStudio takes Schott's (n_d = 1.616592); Optiland and LensHH-LT, from its shipped catalog folder, take CDGM's (1.620047), which moves the wavefront by several waves at the rim. LensHH-LT is shown as gathered with only the Schott catalog (`lenshh-lt-opdc/*_Schott.json`).
- **Optiland's own glass data** departs from the AGF catalogs by up to 8×10⁻⁵ in index on the Cooke triplet and US8264785.
- Optiland is therefore shown traced on OpticStudio's indices: `verification/optiland/points.py --indices` gives every surface a material tabulated with them (`optiland-opdc/*_ZemaxIndices.json`). WEC's `Optiland` preset reproduces those files as it does Optiland on its own glass, to 2.5×10⁻⁸ wave (8.5×10⁻⁶ on US8264785's aspheres).
- OSLO's indices already equal OpticStudio's.

Generated by `sh docs/opd/compare/generate.sh` (from the repository root), which runs `wfe opd-table --values` for each lens and aiming mode, and writes [LensHH-LT_vs_OpticStudio.md](LensHH-LT_vs_OpticStudio.md).

## LensHH-LT and Zemax, ray by ray

LensHH-LT 1.0.161 computes OPDC as OpticStudio defines it: the reference sphere through the primary chief ray's crossing of the paraxial exit pupil plane, crossed exactly on the exit pupil's side. Compared directly at the 857 points and all three wavelengths, on the same glass ([details](LensHH-LT_vs_OpticStudio.md)), the largest |LensHH-LT − OpticStudio| in waves:

| Lens | Aiming off | Aiming real |
|---|---|---|
| Double Gauss (Schott F4) | 1.0×10⁻⁹ | 4.5×10⁻⁷ |
| Cooke triplet | 1.9×10⁻⁹ | 7.8×10⁻⁸ |
| US8264785 | 1.8×10⁻⁸ | 1.3×10⁻⁷; 6.2×10⁻⁶ at 17.5° |
| 1:1 relay | 1.1×10⁻⁹ | 1.1×10⁻⁶ |
| NA 0.3 objective | 5.7×10⁻⁸ | 2.1×10⁻⁷ |

US8264785's 6.2×10⁻⁶ is at the near-grazing rim at full field, where OpticStudio's own values at mirror-image points differ by 7×10⁻⁷. WEC's `Zemax` preset reproduces LensHH-LT to 2.2×10⁻⁸ wave with aiming off and 3.7×10⁻⁷ with aiming real (`LensHHLTParityTests.LensHHLT161IsTheZemaxPreset`).

## The WEC column, checked by integration

The WEC column is computed from optical paths. An independent calculation gives the same numbers from the rays' directions alone: Rayces's exact relation between wave and ray aberration, ∂W/∂x = −X/(R − W) (Optica Acta 11, 85, 1964), integrated across the pupil from the chief ray (`wfe rayces`; method.md §3.4). Largest |integrated − optical path| in waves, over all 857 points of every field, on WEC's reference sphere and on Zemax's:

| Lens | Conjugate | Aiming off | Aiming real |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kingslake double Gauss | infinite | 1.1×10⁻¹⁰ | 9.1×10⁻¹¹ |
| Cooke triplet | infinite | 1.1×10⁻⁹ | 1.1×10⁻⁹ |
| US8264785 | infinite | 6.9×10⁻⁹ | 7.3×10⁻⁹ |
| 1:1 relay | finite | 2.6×10⁻¹⁰ | 3.4×10⁻¹⁰ |
| NA 0.3 objective | finite | 1.3×10⁻¹⁰ | 1.3×10⁻¹⁰ |

So the per-ray values in these tables are exact to a few 10⁻⁹ wave or better, on axis and off: two methods that share nothing but the ray trace agree that far (docs/verification.md).

## Why the columns differ

- **On axis:**
  - Zemax and LensHH-LT match WEC to 2×10⁻⁸ wave with aiming off, and to 2×10⁻⁷ with aiming real.
  - Optiland matches to 6×10⁻⁹, except 3.9×10⁻⁶ on US8264785's aspheres and the objective (below).
  - OSLO matches to 4×10⁻¹⁰ for an object at infinity; the finite-object lenses are below.
- **Off axis, the difference is each program's choice of reference sphere,** not error in its rays.
  - Zemax and LensHH-LT put the sphere through the paraxial chief ray's crossing of the exit pupil plane; WEC puts it through the real chief ray's exit pupil. They part by up to 1.5×10⁻³ wave on the double Gauss at 14°, and 5×10⁻² wave on US8264785 at 17.5°, where W is steepest.
  - WEC reproduces each program under its own conventions (`docs/verification.md`): Zemax to 6×10⁻⁸ wave at every one of these points with aiming off, LensHH-LT to 2.2×10⁻⁸, and Optiland to 2×10⁻⁸ (7.5×10⁻⁶ on US8264785's aspheres) on all but the objective.
- **The objective (NA 0.3, entrance pupil behind the object):**
  - Zemax and LensHH-LT agree with WEC to 3×10⁻⁵ wave.
  - Optiland gives the wavefront with the opposite sign, at (Px, −Py). Its paraxial aimer launches each ray from the object toward the entrance pupil point. That point lies behind the object, so the ray leaves going backward (N < 0) and the pupil's diameter comes out negative.
- **OSLO names its pupil points differently.** At the same (FY, FX) it traces another ray and refers it to another chief ray, so its column differs mostly for that reason, not by a difference in OPD.
  - On axis, for an object at infinity, it matches WEC to 4×10⁻¹⁰ wave.
  - For a finite object of NA 0.1 or more (the relay and the objective), it spaces (FY, FX) evenly in direction sine across the pupil seen from the object, not across the pupil's plane: 6×10⁻³ and 2×10⁻² wave on axis.
  - Off axis, it refers each ray to its reference ray, aimed at the centre of the real stop, not to the paraxial chief ray. On the vignetted Cooke triplet at 20°, that alone moves the wavefront by 0.56 wave.
  - In crr mode it also centres the pupil on that reference ray, so its rays differ further, by up to 0.9 wave on the objective.
  - Under its own conventions (WEC's OSLO switches, `OsloParityTests.OslosOpdAtTheOpdcPoints`), WEC reproduces OSLO at all 857 points to 1.2×10⁻⁸ wave in both modes, with the same 12 rays blocked on US8264785.
  - OSLO gets the objective's sign right.
